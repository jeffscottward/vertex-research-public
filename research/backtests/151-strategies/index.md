## 3. Stocks

|Section|Strategy|Freq.|Wave|Harness|RQ|Rule summary|

|3.1|Price-Momentum|monthly|1|nautilus||Rank stocks each month by past performance over a formation period (usually T=12 months), skipping the most recent S=1 month. Performance can be cumulative return, mean monthly return, or risk-adjusted mean return (mean divided by monthly volatility). Buy the top decile (winners) and short the bottom decile (losers) as a dollar-neutral portfolio, or buy only the top decile for a long-only version. Hold for a set holding period of 1 month or more, using overlapping portfolios for longer holds. Weights are equal (1/N, or ±1/(2N_L) and ±1/(2N_S) for long/short) or proportional to 1/σ or 1/σ².|
|3.2|Earnings-Momentum|event-driven|2|nautilus||For each stock, compute standardized unexpected earnings: SUE = (latest quarterly EPS minus EPS from 4 quarters earlier) divided by the standard deviation of these unexpected earnings over the last 8 quarters. Build a dollar-neutral portfolio that buys stocks in the top SUE decile and shorts stocks in the bottom SUE decile. Rebalance when new earnings are reported.|
|3.3|Value|monthly|2|nautilus||Rank stocks by their book-to-price (B/P) ratio, which is book value per share divided by price (the same as book-to-market). |10.1|Hedging Risk with Futures|event-driven|1|nautilus||A trader who expects to buy (sell) a quantity X of a commodity at a future time T buys (sells) at time t a futures contract with delivery date T for that quantity. This locks in the price and hedges the risk of a price rise (fall) between t and T. The position is held until T.|
|10.1.1|↳ Cross-Hedging|buy-and-hold / static|1|nautilus||To hedge an asset that has no futures contract of its own, the trader holds the asset and shorts a futures contract on a different asset with similar characteristics, keeping the position until maturity T. |10.1.2|↳ Interest Rate Risk Hedging|buy-and-hold / static|1|nautilus||Hedge interest rate risk on a fixed-income position with interest rate futures. |10.2|Calendar Spread|event-driven|2|nautilus|yes|A bull spread buys the nearby futures contract and sells a deferred-month contract on the same underlying. A bear spread does the reverse. The trader opens a bull spread when they expect low supply and high demand, and a bear spread when they expect high supply and low demand. The idea is that nearby commodity contracts react more strongly than deferred contracts to changes in supply and demand.|
|10.3|Contrarian Trading (Mean-Reversion)|weekly|1|nautilus|yes|Compute each future's return over the last week and the equal-weighted market return Rm across N futures. Set weights wi = -γ(Ri - Rm), with γ chosen so the absolute weights sum to 1. This buys the losers and sells the winners relative to the index, and the portfolio is dollar-neutral. Weights may be scaled by 1/σi or 1/σi² (historical volatility) to avoid over-investing in volatile futures. Rebalance weekly.|
|10.3.1|↳ Contrarian Trading—Market Activity|weekly|2|nautilus||Each week, compute the volume factor v = ln(V/V0) for each future, where V is the sum of daily volume over the last week and V0 is the sum over the week before. Compute the open-interest factor u = ln(U/U0) the same way. Keep the futures in the top half by v, then within that set keep the bottom half by u. Apply the basic contrarian rule to this subset: weights equal minus the scaled deviation of each future's last-week return from the cross-sectional mean. This means buying recent losers and shorting recent winners, rebalanced weekly.|
|10.4|Trend Following (Momentum)|monthly|1|nautilus|yes|For each futures contract, compute the cumulative return R_i over a lookback period T (days, weeks or months) and set the signal eta_i = sign(R_i). Optional variants: smooth the signal with eta_i = tanh(R_i/kappa); make the book dollar-neutral by demeaning the weights, or by using separate scale factors gamma+ and gamma- for longs and shorts; or use returns net of the market-index return so that winners are bought and losers are sold relative to the index.|
